Three things most new drivers find out the hard way

01

GST starts with your first fare

The usual $30,000 small-supplier exemption doesn’t apply to rideshare. You’re required to register, charge and remit GST/HST from day one — even part-time.

02

Quiet months still need a return

A GST/HST return is due for every reporting period, even one where you didn’t drive. Missed filings add up fast.

03

The CRA already sees your earnings

Uber and Lyft now report driver earnings to the CRA. Report your income net of the app’s fees instead of gross, and the numbers won’t match — even if your profit is right.

Questions

Is this tax advice?

No. It’s a record-keeping and calculation tool. It organizes your numbers the way the CRA forms ask for them. For advice on your own situation, talk to an accountant — the hand-off page will make that visit quicker.

I drive in Quebec. Will it work?

Not yet. Quebec drivers also deal with QST and Revenu Québec, which work differently. The first version covers the rest of Canada.

I also do food delivery. Is that covered?

The kit is built for rideshare. Delivery income follows different GST rules, so if you do both, have an accountant confirm how to report it. The kit’s hand-off page makes that conversation quick.

How hard is it to set up?

About three minutes. Click one link to copy the kit into your free Google account, then add the shift log to your phone’s home screen. No app to install, and no Excel needed.
